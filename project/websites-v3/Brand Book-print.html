01 / Position

The brand in a sentence.

Before colour, type, or anything visual — this is the centre of gravity. Every artefact should be testable against it.

02 / Voice & tone

Plain. Pointed. Useful.

We sound like a smart operator who's been in the room. Not a consultant. Not a coach. Not a guru. The voice is the same on a £4,700 proposal, on a LinkedIn post, and in a one-line invoice note.

01 / Cadence

Short. Then longer. Then short.

Punch, breathe, punch. Sentence fragments are allowed. Adjectives are taxed. Every paragraph earns its line break.

Direct without being clever.

03 / Wordmark

The mark, the strike, the dot.

Three signals. The two stacked words. The lime strike-through running across each word at a tilt. The lime period after OVER — a stop, not a flourish. Together they read as a logo and a manifesto at the same time.

Anatomy

Two words. One strike. One dot.

CHAOS / OVER stacked, Big Shoulders Display 900. The strike is a 0.04em lime bar at 72% baseline, rotated –39°. The dot is the lime period after OVER. Nothing else.

Clear space

One cap-height on every side.

Use the height of the cap "O" as the unit. Keep that much breathing room around the mark on every side. Never crowd it with rules, gradients, or text.

Minimum size

32px digital · 16mm print.

Below this, the strike-through becomes a smudge. Drop to the inline variant for nav bars, footers and email signatures.

How to mix

The 85 / 10 / 5 rule.

85% bone & sand — body, cards, surface. 10% ink — type, rules, the dark card. ≤5% lime — the strike, the dot, the highlight, the one button you want pressed. If lime fills more than a fifth of the page, something is wrong.

Pastels (lilac + pink) only appear as the fixed parallax gradient behind everything, or as accents inside specific panels (e.g. the Calm Flow retainer block). Never as foreground tints on body copy.

Accessibility

Ink on Bone

15.2 : 1

AAA. The default working pair. Use for all body copy.

Ink on Lime

13.4 : 1

AAA. Why we always put ink type on lime, never lime type on ink.

Lime on Ink

2.7 : 1

Fails AA for body. Lime on dark is decoration only — strikes, dots, hovers, never paragraphs.

Use display when

You want it heard from the next room.

Headlines. Slide titles. The hero strap. The wordmark. Never below 28px — it loses its punch.

Use body when

Someone needs to actually read it.

Proposals, decks, paragraphs, email. Default 16–19px. Be generous with leading. Never set body below 14px.

Use mono when

It's a label, not a sentence.

Eyebrows, prices, dates, references, button text. Never run mono past one line — if it wraps, it's not a label.

06 / Layout

Grid. Air. Hard rules.

Wide containers, tight columns, generous vertical rhythm, and a 1.5px ink rule between sections. The page should feel deliberately spaced — like a print magazine that knows where its edges are.

12-column grid · 40px gutters · 1400px max

Common splits: 7/5 (lede + checklist), 4/4/4 (pillars), 12 (full-bleed band), 3/9 (label + content).

Spacing scale · 8px base

Section padding is always 100–200px vertical. Headlines breathe. Body groups sit tight at 14–24px. Never use values off the scale.

Page edges

40px hard margin.

Same on mobile, tablet, desktop. The cream ground is part of the design — don't fight to reclaim it.

Vertical rhythm

200px between sections.

1.5px solid ink rule across the top of every new section. The rule is the section header — no need for over-decoration.

Cards

32–40px padding.

1px rgba(28,24,34,0.14) border. No drop shadows. No gradients. Surface lifts come from the sand background, not effects.

07 / Signature motifs

The bracket, the highlight, the dot.

Three small devices do most of the brand work. Use them consistently and the brand reads from across the room. Use them indiscriminately and they become wallpaper.

01 / Bracket tag

Mono in square brackets, pastel-lime background, 4px radius. Used for prices, dates, locations, references, side-of-page metadata. The closest thing we have to ornament.

02 / Lime fill

A word that needs to be heard sits in lime.

Gradient-clipped highlight — sits behind the visual letterforms only, not the full em box. One per headline. Never on body copy. Never on more than three words in a row.

03 / The dot

Chaos Over.

A lime full stop at the end of any title, section header, or sign-off. The brand's punctuation. The mark says: that's it. Decision made.

04 / Eyebrow tag

Solid lime block, ink type. The on-page label that introduces a unit of work. Used once at the top of a page or slide — not as repeated decoration.

05 / Strike-through

CHAOS

The –39° lime bar across the wordmark. Reserved for the wordmark only. Never use as a generic underline or strike on other words.

09 / Imagery & ground

The page is the image. Nearly.

We don't lean on stock photography or illustration. The visual richness comes from the typographic system, the cream ground, and the soft parallax gradient behind everything. Where we do use imagery, the rules are tight.

Parallax ground

Fixed background, slow scroll drift. Lilac top-right, pink bottom-left, cream centre. Sits at z-index: -1 behind every page.

Use it when

Anything that lives on screen.

Website, decks shown live, prototypes, social tiles. The drift gives the cream surface depth without ever pulling focus.

Skip it when

Anything that lives on paper.

Printed proposals, invoices, A4/Letter PDFs. Solid bone (#F4EEE2) instead — gradients muddy on print.

Photography

Used sparingly. Always portrait.

One image per long-form artefact at most. Always founder / team / client portrait, never office or laptop stock. Rounded 4px corners, soft shadow, on the dark ink card so it reads as a quoted source.

Illustration

Don't.

No spot illustrations, no abstract shapes, no AI-generated decoration. If a moment needs more energy, reach for type scale or lime contrast — not pictures.

Iconography

One glyph only.

The lime arrow (→) is our entire icon set. Use it for list bullets, button cues, link affordances. Other symbols (×, ●) appear in mono labels but never as primary navigation.

Carousel rules

Max 8 cards.

Open with a punch (bone or ink). Middle in ink for weight. Close on lime for the payoff. Never two lime cards in a row.

Density

One headline. No body.

If the caption needs explaining, it goes in the LinkedIn post copy — not on the card. Cards are statements, not slides.

Typography

36–56px Big Shoulders.

Tracking –2%, leading 0.88–0.95. Same headline rules as the website. Don't shrink type to fit more words; cut the words.
